This is the one proposal we bring, and it stays GRR's call. Single personas are too small to measure every month, so the tier works on segments: broader groups that combine the deterioration family, whether we can reach the customer, and whether credibility or ability is Moderate or High. That gives about 20 to 27 segments. For each segment we learn from past months the average money collected per account in the next three months, and GRR sets the cut-offs from the in-house cost and the agency commission; every account then takes its segment's tier. At illustrative cut-offs on cards, the in-house tier would hold 17 percent of accounts and 48 percent of the money; the agency tier 54 percent and 46 percent; and the bottom 29 percent, with only 6 percent of the money, matches the policy's non-workable pool. These shares are of accounts, not of the balance, and come from our first look, where the tier was set per persona; set per segment, the in-house tier holds the same 17 percent of card accounts and catches as much of the money. They are not the 46 personas on the earlier slide: that group was picked by a rule, reachable with High or Moderate credibility, while the tier is picked by money collected and also takes customers who are able. Loans keep the same order, with fewer accounts. Today effort is spread almost evenly: card accounts get about 11 call days in three months in every tier, the non-workable pool included. And on segments the tier is stable: only 1.2 percent of card accounts and 4.5 percent of loan accounts change tier from month to month while their persona stays the same; a tier list frozen for three months lost no visible share of the money. GRR can show the tier on the card, test it, or leave it for later.
